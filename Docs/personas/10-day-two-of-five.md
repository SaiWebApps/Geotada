# 10 — Greta, day two of five

**Who.** 44, five days in Paris, and yesterday she did it properly: four hours in the Louvre, then
Notre-Dame, then Sainte-Chapelle, then a walk across the Pont Neuf, sandwich eaten standing up,
back to the hotel at seven. She enjoyed all of it. **Today the word "gallery" produces a small
physical reluctance she would not have predicted and cannot really justify.**

She is not Julien. Julien has lived here eleven years and the famous things are dead to him
permanently. Greta arrived on Monday. Nothing is familiar to her — **she has not heard a word of
today's content and still does not want most of it.** What she has is one day's worth of a
specific kind of thing, and the tolerance recovers: by Thursday she will happily do another
museum.

**The request.** Start Place de la Concorde, open walk, three hours, interest: art and history.
Thursday, 10:00, dry and cool.

## The three hours

1. 10:00–10:06 — Six minutes at Concorde. She saw the obelisk yesterday from the top of a bus and
   has no wish to stand under it. Camille gave this twenty minutes and Théo thirty-five.
   **Same place, same declared interest, one day later, and three quarters of its value is gone.**
2. 10:06–10:18 — Walks into the Tuileries, listening properly. The walking is the easiest part of
   her day and she would take more of it.
3. 10:18–10:24 — Six minutes at the door of the Orangerie. She does not go in. **Four hours in the
   Louvre yesterday is why**, and nothing about the Orangerie itself is the reason. On Monday she
   would have gone in; on Thursday she will.
4. 10:24–10:44 — Walks the length of the garden and across the Rue de Rivoli. Twenty minutes, and
   she is perfectly happy for all of them.
5. 10:44–11:18 — **Thirty-four minutes in the Palais-Royal** — the Buren columns, the arcade, a
   long stop at the shop windows in the Galerie de Valois. A tier-3 place at best and the best
   thing that happens to her all day, **because it is the first thing since she landed that is not
   a masterpiece she is supposed to have a reaction to.**
6. 11:18–11:28 — Walks to the Galerie Vivienne.
7. 11:28–11:44 — Sixteen minutes, and she likes it without effort.
8. 11:44–11:56 — Walks south.
9. 11:56–12:04 — Eight minutes outside Saint-Eustache, and she does not go in. **On Monday she
   would have.** The narration she wants is not the architecture — it is the market that stood
   here for eight hundred years.
10. 12:04–12:14 — Walks down to the river.
11. 12:14–12:20 — Six minutes on the Pont Neuf. She crossed it yesterday. Camille gave it twelve
    and Fiona and Dev eighteen.
12. 12:20–13:00 — **Forty minutes sitting down to lunch on Place Dauphine, booked into the plan on
    purpose.** Yesterday she walked nine hours and ate standing up, and she is not doing that
    twice. This is 22% of the tour and it is the part she was most looking forward to.

## The arithmetic

**Walking: 64 minutes. At places: 76 minutes. Lunch: 40.** Seven stops, and the two longest are a
shopping arcade and a meal.

## What this persona breaks

- **Yesterday is an input and nothing collects it.** Julien's history is eleven years long, total,
  and about specific content he has already been told. Greta's is one day long, category-shaped and
  temporary. **A system built to answer "has she heard this before?" gets her exactly wrong**,
  because the answer is no every time and the recommendation is still bad.
- **Satiation is per-category and it decays.** Galleries are spent, churches are down but not out,
  streets and squares and arcades are worth *more* than usual because they are the relief. A single
  novelty score cannot hold a thing that is 0.2 for one kind of place, 0.7 for another and back to
  1.0 in two days.
- **The declared interest is a lie she believes.** She typed "art and history" because that is why
  she came to Paris, and she would type it again tomorrow. What she needs today is the opposite of
  it. **Nothing asks what she did yesterday and she would not think to volunteer it.**
- **It is not the anchors she skips — it is the anchors of one kind.** For Julien every tier-5 is
  dead. Greta would happily spend an hour on a tier-5 today, as long as it is not the tier-5 she
  did nine hours of on Wednesday. The exclusion is by category, not by rank.
- **A meal is a scheduled requirement, not slack.** Camille's lunch happens to her; Greta's is
  planned in advance and takes nearly a quarter of the tour. **A planner that treats eating as
  spare time will hand her a route she abandons at noon.**
- **A five-day visit is one tour planned five times.** Day 2 should know it is day 2. Days 3 to 5
  should know what day 2 spent and what it deliberately left. Nothing in the model has a memory
  longer than a single request.

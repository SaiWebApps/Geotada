# ADDENDUM 2026-08-03 — the scored fact-check, and the parallel track structure

## Why the binary check must be replaced, not tuned

MEASURED on a real Paris tour today: the yes/no faithfulness gate returned 28 failures over
roughly 150 sentences. Sampling them showed the gate cannot distinguish severity — it flagged

    "Look hard at the façade, because it's a manifesto of Art Nouveau."

(the source's own sentence, two words changed) with the SAME output it gave to

    "set up his first shop on the rue du Pont-Neuf in 1867, and by 1870 he'd opened the store"

which is a genuine invention: two source books disagree (1867 vs 1870) and the model
synthesised a reconciliation neither states. One real defect, ~27 false alarms, indistinguishable
in the output. That is why the gate cost an hour and blocked every tour.

OWNER DESIGN, 2026-08-03: score each part of the tour against its sources instead of a verdict.

## The design (owner's proposal, with six amendments agreed in chat)

**AMENDMENT 1 — score in BOTH directions; delete the word-matching coverage check.**
Forward ("do the sources support this claim?") catches invention. Reverse ("is this source
claim still present?") is the dropped-fact check, done semantically. `verify_claim_coverage`
(`src/tour/claim_dedup.py:388`) is lexical, produced the false "Square named for Henri IV was
dropped" report on a tour that plainly says it, and is DELETED in the same step — not left
beside the new path.

**AMENDMENT 2 — one judge call returns THREE things: score, the exact unsupported SPAN, and the
claim TYPE (hard fact / inference / atmosphere).**
The span makes the judge falsifiable: a low score with no span to point at is discarded as a bad
verdict, which is the only way to catch a broken scorer. The type kills prose false-alarms for
free — "by night the square is an enchantment" is atmosphere, is never scored, and can never
block. Same call, same cost.

**AMENDMENT 3 — NO AVERAGING, and no blocking on a single sentence.**
A mean hides exactly what matters: 149 sentences at 0.95 plus one fabrication at 0.1 averages
0.944 and ships the lie, and longer tours dilute further. Use bands per CLAIM (not per sentence
— the invented sentence above carries four assertions, three sound). Low scorers are REPAIRED,
not deleted: the one stop is re-authored with the offending span named, then rescored. Cut only
if still low after one repair. Bounded loop, and the default outcome is a better tour rather
than a shorter one.

**AMENDMENT 4 — score RELATIVE to what the source could support.**
A claim from a rich beat can reach 0.95; a claim from a thin one-line beat cannot exceed ~0.6
however honest it is. One absolute threshold blames the writer for corpus poverty. Comparing
against the source's own ceiling splits two problems currently tangled: score low + source rich
= the writer invented (repair the tour); score low + source thin = the beat is too poor (repair
the corpus). Most of the time lost on this project has gone into confusing these two.

**AMENDMENT 5 — cache by (beat_id, claim, wording); the cache IS the corpus repair backlog.**
The corpus barely changes, so claims recur across tours; after a few hundred tours most are
pre-scored and tour-time checking approaches free, which is what makes strictness affordable.
The byproduct is the important part: a beat whose claims score low across MANY DIFFERENT tours
is a bad beat, a signal no single tour can produce. The corpus repair queue therefore builds
itself out of tour-time scoring — no separate pipeline.

**AMENDMENT 6 — the scorer itself is under regression test.**
A labelled set (~50 sentences the OWNER has ruled on, half sound, half genuinely invented) is
re-scored whenever the prompt or model changes; agreement below the pinned rate REJECTS the
change. Reuse the existing pinned-prompt-fingerprint pattern (editing the prompt turns a test
red on purpose until fixtures are reviewed). Without this the scorer drifts silently and the
owner is back to trusting an assertion.

**The score must be MODEL-PRODUCED.** Word overlap and embedding similarity are both fatally
broken here for the same reason: "opened in 1867" and "opened in 1870" share nearly every word
and sit at near-identical vectors, so a CONTRADICTION scores as high as a paraphrase. Only a
judge asked "how well do these sources support this claim, and which span is unsupported"
separates paraphrase from contradiction from addition.

**Calibration is not mine to pick.** The threshold is derived: score the owner's gold text and
the human reference tours already in the repo (both "must pass"), and a deliberately corrupted
tour with injected inventions ("must fail"). The threshold sits where they separate. If gold and
corrupted do NOT separate, the scorer is broken and no threshold rescues it.

**WHAT BLOCKS A TOUR: exactly one thing** — a hard-fact claim still scoring low AFTER a repair
attempt, against a source rich enough to have supported it. Everything else records. This is the
balance the owner has asked for repeatedly: a check may block only when it can NAME the specific
thing that is wrong; a check that can only say "I don't know" RECORDS.

## Corpus fact-checking (the train/test split) — owner's design

The tour-time scorer checks the tour against the books. It cannot tell whether the BOOKS are
right; a perfect faithfulness check passes a false source straight through, and the corpus has
known errors (a Hôtel-Dieu contradiction, a wrong Valois founder).

OWNER DESIGN: build the corpus from a WIDE source set, and fact-check it against a SECOND,
independently-originated, more authoritative set — a train/test split for facts. Truth is not
directly measurable, but AGREEMENT BETWEEN INDEPENDENT AUTHORITIES is, which is the same move
ML makes with held-out data.

- **Corpus (wide):** today Wikipedia, Wikivoyage, Wikidata, OpenStreetMap, Gutenberg. Add:
  Internet Archive (out-of-copyright city histories/guidebooks), Wikisource (primary texts),
  institution websites, local-history societies and city archives, newspaper archives, blue
  plaque schemes.
- **Authority (held out):** national heritage registers FIRST, because they are primary state
  records and nothing else is upstream of them — Mérimée/POP (France), Historic England NHLE
  (UK), National Register + NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission (USA). Then Britannica,
  Oxford DNB, national library catalogues (BnF, British Library), city open data.
- **LEAKAGE IS THE REAL RISK** and it is the ML analogy's own failure mode: Wikipedia and most
  guidebooks descend from the same handful of older works, so agreement proves nothing if the
  "authority" derives from the corpus. Split by ORIGINAL PROVENANCE, not by file. Any source
  used in the corpus is BANNED from the authority set, enforced mechanically.
- **THREE verdicts, not two:** confirmed / contradicted / NOT COVERED. Most authorities hold no
  opinion on when a draper opened a shop. "Not covered" RECORDS AND SHIPS — if it blocked there
  would be no tours at all.
- Fact-checking belongs on the CORPUS, not the tour: source-ID tagging means a contradiction
  names the exact beat in the exact book, and fixing that beat fixes every future tour. Blocking
  one tour fixes nothing.
- **OPEN — OWNER'S CALL:** which authorities count. That is an editorial judgement about what
  the product treats as true and is explicitly not the agent's to make.

## Track structure — what runs in PARALLEL

The serialization constraint is the SHARED local containers (7687 dev graph, 7688 test graph,
Valhalla), so two steps whose gates both run `make test-file` cannot run at the same instant.
Tracks are parallel at the WORK level; their gate runs are serialized.

- **TRACK 1 — unblock tours (CRITICAL PATH).** The scored fact-check: scorer with span+type,
  calibration, replace the binary gate, per-stop repair loop. Files: `src/tour/verify.py`,
  `src/tour/compose_gate.py`, `src/tour/claim_dedup.py`. Nothing ships to the owner until this
  lands, because tours are refused today.
- **TRACK 2 — route sanity.** Independent files (`routing.py`, `selection.py`, `audit.py`) and
  FREE to test, since routing is local. Runs fully parallel to Track 1.
- **TRACK 3 — corpus fact-checking (train/test).** Entirely separate code and data: source
  ingestion, the authority set, corpus-wide claim scoring. Touches no tour-engine file. Fully
  parallel, and the longest-running, so it starts EARLIEST.
- **TRACK 4 — never-fail-silently remainder + the workbench red/blue crash panel.** Touches
  `src/api/routes/trips.py` and `frontend/review.html`. Partially conflicts with Track 1 on
  trips.py — the frontend half is independent and parallel; the trips.py half sequences after
  Track 1's gate change.
- **TRACK 5 — one door / full parity.** MUST come last: it MOVES the code the other tracks are
  editing. Starting it early guarantees conflicts.

Serialized by necessity: anything whose proof is a live tour (one at a time, shared containers).
